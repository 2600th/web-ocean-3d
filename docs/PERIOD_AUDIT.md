# Period audit: web-ocean-3d (Caribbean c. 1690-1730), branch aaa-pass

Read-only audit. Verdicts: **yes** = fits 1690-1730, **doubtful** = passes at distance or has a better stand-in, **anachronistic** = does not belong.

Provenance notes:
- `dressing-manifest.json` holds LOD URLs only (no source/licence). Provenance is in `ASSET_LICENSES.md` and in the Poly Haven API tags/categories (fetched from `api.polyhaven.com/info/<slug>`).
- Poly Haven's "collection: smugglers_cove" covers the ship, pinnace, cannon, fort, pier, barrels, bucket, crates, lantern and chest, so those were authored as a period pirate set.
- `ASSET_LICENSES.md` (~lines 200-212) still credits Sketchfab "Royal Poinciana", "Hong Kong Orchid Tree", "Coconut Palm" and "Tropical Palm". No such glb exists in `public/` and flora is now procedural (`src/scene/flora/`), so those credits are stale. The comment at `Props.ts` ~1455 ("the orchid tree over most of the flanks") is stale too. The Hong Kong orchid tree (Bauhinia x blakeana) dates from c.1880.
- Source names below are Wikipedia article titles, museum or collection names, or Poly Haven's own metadata. I did not open the cited pages in this session; verify any date you plan to quote in-game.

## D. Period props worth adding (procedural recipes)

- **Admiralty-pattern anchor** (the only type before the stockless anchor of 1821; Wikipedia: "Anchor"). Long iron shank with a ring at the top; a wooden stock (two timbers bound with iron straps) crossing the shank just below the ring, 90 degrees to the arms; two curved arms ending in broad triangular flukes, arm span about 60-70% of shank length. Tarred hemp cable. Use at the bow cathead for the gameplay anchor, plus one beached as a prop.
- **Casks** (Wikipedia: "Barrel"). Lathe profile with bilge diameter about 1.25x the head, 8-12 hoops (iron or split ash), stave seams, bung at the bilge. Sizes: barrel about 0.6 m, tierce about 0.75 m, hogshead about 1.0 m, puncheon about 1.2 m, pipe/butt about 1.4 m. Add a rope sling and a chalk or brand mark.
- **Sea chest** (Wikipedia: "Sea chest"). Flat-topped box, wider at the base than the top, rope beckets at the sides, hasp and padlock, iron corner straps, oiled or painted lid.
- **Spanish iron-banded strongbox (arca).** Domed lid, wide forged straps, large escutcheon lock; for the reef find.
- **Swivel gun** (Wikipedia: "Swivel gun"). Short iron or bronze barrel (about 0.7-1.0 m) with a trunnion fork on a post that sockets into the rail, tiller bar at the breech. Versus a **carriage gun**: long iron barrel (1.8-2.5 m) with trunnions on a wooden truck carriage (four small solid wheels, stepped cheeks, quoin).
- **Careening camp** (Wikipedia: "Careening"). A hull heeled on a beach with the keel exposed, held by tackles to trees or a sunk anchor; sailcloth tents (the existing lean-to), a tar kettle on a fire, bundles of reeds or palm for breaming, wooden scrapers and brooms, a tallow pot, planks as a work platform, a spare spar, a ship's boat drawn up on the sand.
- **Boucan (smoke rack)** (Wikipedia: "Buccaneer"). Four forked posts with a lattice of green sticks 1 m above a smouldering fire, strips of meat hung on it; the origin of "buccaneer".
- **Palm-thatch ajoupa hut.** Leaning pole frame, overlapping palm leaves, open sides.
- **Turtle crawl.** A shallow stake pen with turtles turned on their backs on the sand.
- **Capstan** (drum with 6-8 bar sockets) and **belaying pins** on the dock or deck.
- **Rope coils** (tube swept on a helix, tarred brown-black), a **hammock** (cloth sag between two posts), and **tarred bollards** on the jetty.
- **Spyglass, hourglass, brass dividers and chart** on a dock table.
- **Flintlock pistol** and **hanger** with scabbard.
- **Case bottles, Bartmann jugs and a Spanish olive jar** as camp or wreck debris; **pieces of eight** and silver bars as the reef find.
- **Fixed-light lighthouse** for the world scene: masonry tower with slight batter, gallery with iron rail, glazed lantern or open chauffer fire with smoke, flickering warm point light.
